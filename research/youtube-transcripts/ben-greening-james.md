## Post Topic (Strategic Theme)

A systematic, five-step LinkedIn outbound framework for B2B SaaS and 
service businesses to consistently book 3–5 qualified sales meetings per 
week — without spamming, automation blasting, or pitching on first contact. 
The core philosophy: warm prospects over 14 days across multiple channels 
before ever asking for a meeting.

---

## Key Tactics (Frameworks & 'How-To')

### 1. Profile as a Landing Page

Your LinkedIn profile is the first conversion barrier. Treat it like a 
dedicated landing page, not a résumé:

- Use a clear, professional headshot — not a lifestyle photo
- Design a banner image that communicates your offer immediately
- Add a direct CTA link (booking page or lead magnet) in the Featured section
- Write a headline that clearly states what you do and who you help
- Study competitor profiles or top performers in your niche for inspiration

### 2. Build Your "Hit 100" Ideal Client List

Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator with precise Boolean search filters to build 
a tightly qualified prospect list. Key filter criteria include:

| Filter               | Example                              |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Job Title            | Boolean string (include/exclude)     |
| Years of Experience  | 1–10+ years                         |
| Location             | City or region                       |
| Company Headcount    | 1–50 (SMB targeting)                 |
| Industry             | Specific vertical                    |
| Recent Activity      | Posted on LinkedIn recently          |

> **Warning:** A poorly filtered list poisons every downstream step. 
> Validate filters manually before running any outreach sequence.

### 3. Set Up a Tracking System (CRM or Google Sheet)

Track every prospect across both outreach channels. Minimum fields to log:

- Company name, first name, email address
- LinkedIn profile URL
- Outreach channel used (LinkedIn + secondary channel)
- Response status and call outcome
- Notes on fit and conversation context

This enables you to identify what's working, double down on it, and 
eliminate what isn't — rather than repeating failed messaging blindly.

### 4. The 14-Day Outreach Sequence

The core of the system. Spread outreach over 14 days across LinkedIn 
(primary) and a secondary channel (email, X/Twitter, etc.). The goal is 
to sound and look human, establish a credible reason to connect, and only 
pitch after trust has been initiated.

#### Full Cadence Breakdown:

| Day    | Action                                         | Notes                                      |
|--------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Day 1  | Send connection request (blank)                | No pitch, no "like-minded individuals"     |
| Day 2  | Engage with profile (view or like a post)      | Triggers a notification to the prospect    |
| Day 3  | Send soft opener message                       | "Good to connect, [Name]" — nothing more   |
| Day 5  | Send trigger message (the real outreach)       | Reference a specific, relevant reason      |
| Day 6  | Engage with profile again                      | Resurfaces your name in their notifications|
| Day 7  | Follow up on secondary channel (email/X)       | Reference the LinkedIn message briefly     |
| Day 8  | Wait / engage with profile                     | Let the sequence breathe                   |
| Day 9  | Send "forgot to mention" message               | Add a second value point naturally         |
| Day 11 | Standard nudge (non-pushy)                     | Check in, keep it light                    |
| Day 12 | Engage with profile                            | Keep name visible                          |
| Day 13 | Final LinkedIn "breaker" message               | Give opt-in or opt-out gracefully          |
| Day 14 | Final email breaker                            | Close the loop — many replies come here    |

#### Trigger Message Examples (Day 5):

- **Mutual connection:** "Saw we're both connected with John — we helped 
  him generate 50 leads last month. Is this something you're exploring?"
- **Case study:** "We just wrapped up with [similar company] and delivered 
  [result]. Would you be open to seeing how this could work for you?"
- **Hiring trigger:** "Noticed you're hiring a BDM — I run a team that 
  replaces the need for an internal hire and typically 2x the output. 
  Open to a quick chat?"

> **Rule:** Never pitch in the connection request or the first message. 
> The trigger message (Day 5) is the first time you explain *why* you're 
> reaching out.

### 5. Turning Conversations into Calls

Once a prospect engages, convert efficiently without being pushy:

- **Qualify first** — confirm they have the pain point and urgency before 
  pushing for a meeting
- **Offer two specific time options** instead of a Calendly link:
  > "Are you free Tuesday at 2pm or Wednesday at 4pm?"
- If those don't work, prospects will suggest their own time
- Only send a calendar link if you know your prospect is tech-comfortable
- Goal: make booking a call the path of least resistance

## Why It Works for B2B SaaS

B2B SaaS sales cycles require trust before commitment. This framework 
succeeds because it mirrors how real professional relationships develop:

- **Gradual warm-up** prevents the prospect from mentally categorising you 
  as a spammer before you've had a chance to demonstrate relevance
- **Trigger-based outreach** makes each message feel contextual and 
  research-backed rather than mass-blasted — critical in a landscape where 
  LinkedIn inboxes are saturated with generic automation
- **Multi-channel follow-up** captures prospects who are active on 
  LinkedIn inconsistently — a common reality for busy SaaS buyers and 
  decision-makers
- **Breaker messages** recover late-stage interest from prospects who were 
  genuinely interested but distracted — a significant source of missed 
  pipeline for most outbound teams

## Tactical Takeaways

- **Never pitch in the first message.** Wait until Day 5 with a 
  trigger-based reason to reach out — this single change dramatically 
  improves response rates versus automated blast approaches.

- **Always use a secondary follow-up channel.** LinkedIn inboxes are 
  noisy and unreliable. Pairing LinkedIn with email (or X/Twitter for 
  tech founders) significantly increases total meetings booked from the 
  same lead list.

- **Use semi-automation, not full automation.** Tools like Prosp handle 
  the mechanical heavy lifting (connection requests, profile views, 
  engagement) — but all actual replies and conversation management should 
  be done manually to maintain quality and avoid getting flagged.

- **Track everything from day one.** A Google Sheet is sufficient to start. 
  Without tracking, you cannot identify which triggers, ICPs, or message 
  variants are driving results — and you'll repeat failures indefinitely.

- **Lead with an opt-out in breaker messages.** Giving prospects a graceful 
  way to disengage ("no worries if not relevant") paradoxically increases 
  response rates — it removes pressure and prompts previously-interested 
  but distracted prospects to finally reply.
