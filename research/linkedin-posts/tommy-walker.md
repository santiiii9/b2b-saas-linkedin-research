# Tommy Walker — LinkedIn Research Summary

**Profile:** [Tommy Walker](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tommyismyname) — Founder of [The Content Studio](https://www.thecontentstudio.com/). Former first marketing hire at Shopify Plus, Global Editor-in-Chief at QuickBooks. Teaches B2B marketers to apply screenwriting and narrative design to content programs.

**Research focus:** B2B SaaS LinkedIn organic growth tactics, content distribution frameworks, and audience building/positioning.

**Date collected:** June 19, 2026

**Note:** Walker's LinkedIn content rarely discusses platform mechanics (algorithm, posting frequency, etc.). His organic growth approach is **narrative design applied to B2B content** — using personal storytelling on LinkedIn to teach frameworks that SaaS teams can apply across LinkedIn and their full content ecosystem.

---

## Executive Summary

Tommy Walker's recent LinkedIn activity centers on one thesis: **B2B SaaS content fails when it treats assets as isolated deliverables instead of a connected narrative.** His recurring frameworks are:

1. **Cast Design** — Replace static personas with the social system around a buyer (allies, antagonists, gatekeepers, skeptics).
2. **8-Layer Market Research** — Build differentiated positioning and distribution *before* creating content.
3. **Narrative Design / 4-Act Calendar** — Structure quarterly content like a TV season with super-objectives, central conflicts, and role-specific assets.
4. **Competing Values + Asset Roles** — Give every piece a single narrative job (set context, build urgency, help champions forward internally, etc.).

His own LinkedIn posts model the tactic: casual personal hooks (mechanic story, failed exec pitch) that make complex strategy accessible and shareable.

### 2. Most Personas Are Fan Fiction — Use Cast Design Instead

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_most-personas-are-just-fan-fiction-theyre-activity-7317602348518825984-vE5N) (~1 year ago)

**Post Topic:** Audience building and positioning for B2B SaaS — replacing persona documents with **cast design** to reflect how buying actually happens inside companies.

**Key Tactics:**
- Challenge the assumption that B2B buying is rational and maps cleanly to persona docs.
- Map the reader's world as a story: who they must win over, who blocks them, who they trust/fear/report to.
- Use cast roles (allies, antagonists, mentors) to inform tone, structure, and message.
- Aim for **forwardable** content — pieces a champion can share internally as "exactly what I needed to say."
- Workshop CTA: map allies, antagonists, and mentors; write content that fuels internal conversations.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS deals involve buying committees, security reviews, and hidden blockers (commenters cite IT security leads killing deals early). Personas miss these dynamics.
- Content that names the reader's internal politics becomes a sales enablement asset — shared in Slack, not just consumed in the feed.
- Shifts positioning from "we understand your job title" to "we understand your organizational reality," which builds trust faster in complex B2B sales cycles.

### 3. "Write for One Person" Is Terrible Advice

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_write-for-one-person-its-terrible-advice-activity-7310304748358782976-Yi_z) (~1 year ago)

**Post Topic:** Audience building for B2B SaaS — designing a **cast of characters** around the reader instead of a single persona.

**Key Tactics:**
- Define six cast roles: Allies, Antagonists, Mentors, Gatekeepers, Sidekicks, Skeptics/Tempters.
- Weight arguments by representing real roles in the reader's professional life.
- For buying-committee products, make each stakeholder a "character" so content gets forwarded to the right people.
- Frame goal as **conversation starter, not monologue** — prompt comments asking readers to name roles that affect their deals.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS champions must sell internally. Content designed for their cast gives them scripts for private channels (Slack, email forwards).
- Organic LinkedIn growth for SaaS often comes from *distribution inside accounts*, not viral reach. Cast-aware content is built to travel.
- Engagement bait ("who's in your reader's cast?") drives comments that surface real buying dynamics — free research for future content.

### 4. What Is Cast Design? (Breaking Bad Framework)

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_what-is-cast-design-this-will-give-your-activity-7311111229962354689-sxUP) (~1 year ago)

**Post Topic:** Deep-dive audience positioning — applying Robert McKee's screenwriting cast functions to B2B content strategy.

**Key Tactics:**
- Assign functional roles to supporting characters: Ally, Antagonist, Mentor, Gatekeeper, Sidekick, Skeptic — each reveals something about the protagonist.
- Illustrate with *Breaking Bad* cast mapping, then translate to a content marketer's real world (SEO lead as ally, Finance as antagonist, Legal as gatekeeper, Manager as skeptic).
- Combine cast design with **Jungian archetypes** for the primary reader to play with relationship dynamics in content.
- Research goal: discover how ideal customers perceive their own cast, then reflect it realistically in content.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS content often speaks to one role (e.g., VP Marketing) while Finance, Legal, or IT hold veto power. Cast design forces multi-stakeholder messaging.
- Using familiar TV references makes abstract B2B strategy memorable on LinkedIn — increasing saves, shares, and workshop signups.
- Demonstrates category positioning: Walker isn't another "LinkedIn tips" creator; he's a narrative strategist — a differentiated niche in crowded SaaS content advice.

### 5. The 8 Layers of Market Research

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_the-8-layers-of-market-research-activity-7231359357051940866-LzAj) | [LinkedIn Article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/8-layers-market-research-tommy-walker-xi8he) (~8–12 months ago)

**Post Topic:** Content distribution and differentiation framework — research layers that prevent generic B2B SaaS content and build distribution before publishing.

**Key Tactics:**
- Run eight research layers in tandem:
  1. **You** — core brand narratives beyond the brand team's silo
  2. **Your customers** — shared "DNA" spark, not persona demographics
  3. **Your competitors** — cancel out sameness (logos, buzzwords); find substance
  4. **Search competitors** — treat SERP neighbors as potential partners
  5. **Industry adjacent** — apps/agencies targeting the same customer
  6. **News** — timeliness for digital PR and references
  7. **Influencers** — who's driving conversation; partnership opportunities
  8. **Pulse** — 30-minute daily scan to feed company and content
- Use layers to build internal connections, distribution networks, differentiated messaging, and launch hedges.
- LinkedIn CTA pattern: bookmark, repost, comment — designed for save-and-share utility posts.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS content commoditization is extreme (same AI-slop, same "digital transformation" framing). This framework systematically finds white space.
- Layer 4–7 directly address **distribution** — the biggest organic growth failure mode is "publish and pray."
- Breaking silos (Layer 1) mirrors SaaS reality where product, sales, CS, and marketing each hold narrative fragments — unifying them creates coherent positioning.

### 6. Three Lessons After Publishing Thousands of Pieces

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_ive-published-thousands-of-pieces-of-content-activity-7337852835734110210-RnBK) (~1 year ago)

**Post Topic:** Content distribution framework — building a cohesive content ecosystem instead of disconnected assets (blogs, podcasts, webinars).

**Key Tactics:**
- **Lesson 1 — Narrative throughline:** Connect all assets to one story. Build up to webinar problems with blog posts; use video for process; podcasts for outside voices. Everything serves the narrative.
- **Lesson 2 — Competing values:** Distill stories to "this vs. that" (Freedom vs. Security, Simplicity vs. Sophistication). Zoom out from tactics to emotional stakes and tradeoffs buyers face.
- **Lesson 3 — Asset role clarity:** Each piece does one job — set context, build urgency, shift belief, help champions make the internal case, or close the loop. Calendar becomes a story arc, not a deliverable backlog.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS teams produce volume across blog, LinkedIn, email, and sales enablement without connective tissue. This framework increases return visits, shortens sales cycles, and improves LTV (as Walker cites).
- Competing values create **point-of-view positioning** — essential for SaaS categories where feature parity is high.
- Role-specific assets map to B2B buyer journey: TOFU context → MOFU belief shift → BOFU champion enablement.

### 7. Narrative Design in 4 (Not So Easy) Steps

**Source:** [The Studio Insider Newsletter](https://newsletter.thecontentstudio.com/posts/narrative-design-in-4-not-so-easy-steps) (promoted via LinkedIn articles/posts, ~7–8 months ago) | Related: [Content Calendar Lightning Lesson](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_stuck-figuring-out-what-your-content-calendar-activity-7273436430700343297-gYHk)

**Post Topic:** Content distribution framework for B2B SaaS — quarterly narrative calendars using screenwriting structure (example: Head of Onboarding at a fast-growing SaaS).

**Key Tactics:**
- **Step 1 — Super-Objective:** Define the customer's big, seemingly impossible goal from their perspective (e.g., transform onboarding from cost center to revenue engine).
- **Step 2 — Central Conflict:** Name the systemic antagonist (e.g., siloed GTM motion treating post-sale as afterthought) — often internal, not a competitor.
- **Step 3 — Four Acts (quarters):** Map journey phases (Chaos → Clarity → Consistency → Compounding → Culture) as quarterly themes.
- **Step 4 — Monthly breakdown:** Each act splits into 3 monthly objectives/conflicts; weekly topics form a logical narrative progression across formats (article, video, podcast, workshop).
- **Measure narrative** via recall, resonance (message mirroring), repeat engagement, narrative momentum (multi-asset sessions), sales velocity, and CLTV — not just MQL volume.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- Replaces ad-hoc "what should we post this week?" with "where are we in the story?" — critical for lean SaaS marketing teams.
- System1 research Walker cites: narratively consistent brands see double profit gains; inconsistent brands spend 1.75x more to keep pace.
- SaaS onboarding/customer success narratives are high-stakes and committee-driven — the worked example (Rocketlane-style persona) is directly transferable.

### 8. Leadership Doesn't "Get" Content — Maybe We Don't Get Leadership

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_ok-maybe-this-one-is-a-little-bit-on-us-activity-7240383702680170496-V4ZT) (~9 months ago)

**Post Topic:** B2B SaaS organic content strategy enablement — earning leadership buy-in so narrative-driven programs actually ship.

**Key Tactics:**
- Reframe content marketing using leaders' own behavior (The Office binge-watching analogy: buyers don't convert on one blog post; they need repeated exposure).
- When pitching programs, include: real-world sentiment behind the idea, **pre-confirmed distribution outlets**, cumulative campaign reach, expected outcomes/benchmarks, and time/resource requirements.
- Speak leadership's risk language ("hedging bets") instead of creative vision alone.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS organic programs die in leadership meetings, not in content quality. This post addresses the #1 blocker for sustained LinkedIn + content investment.
- Pre-selling distribution (Layer 7–8 of his research model) de-risks programs — Walker learned this after the failed hub pitch (see Post 9).
- TV analogies make long-term content ROI intuitive to non-marketer executives who approve SaaS marketing budgets.

### 9. The Biggest Content Marketing Mistake (Failed Hub Pitch)

**Source:** [LinkedIn Post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tommyismyname_the-biggest-content-marketing-mistake-i-ever-activity-7387173039978938368-YGUf) (~4 months ago)

**Post Topic:** Content distribution and program design — why ambitious multi-channel hubs fail without ROI framing, and how to restructure for measurable organic growth.

**Key Tactics:**
- Story-driven post mortem of a multimedia hub pitch (video, podcasts, blogs, ebooks, live events) that collapsed when execs asked: cost, timeline, lead volume impact.
- **What he'd do differently:** Scope cost/time upfront (~$1M+ over 18 months); reframe ROI around repeat visitors, email capture from live experiences/ebooks, SQL quality, and close-rate improvement — with researched benchmarks.
- Shift from "creative vision" pitch to **operator pitch** with metrics leadership expects.

**Why it works for B2B SaaS:**
- SaaS teams frequently over-index on content volume and under-index on distribution + measurement — Walker's public failure is a cautionary template.
- Comments section expands the lesson: agree on success definition *before* the pitch (leads vs. retention vs. problem awareness).
- Models LinkedIn organic growth via **vulnerable founder-story content** — high engagement, trust-building, and implicit authority.

| Theme | Walker's Core Argument | SaaS Application |

| **Organic growth** | Teach named frameworks via personal stories; make content forwardable and conversation-starting | Founders/PMMs post cast-design and competing-values content that champions share internally |

## Key Frameworks Reference

1. **Cast Design Roles:** Ally, Antagonist, Mentor, Gatekeeper, Sidekick, Skeptic/Tempter
2. **8 Layers:** You → Customers → Competitors → Search Competitors → Industry Adjacent → News → Influencers → Pulse

4. **Asset Roles:** Set context / Build urgency / Shift belief / Enable champion / Close loop
